Sheffield's Declining Performance on Standard 23

A measurable decline threatens patient care pathways

Sheffield PH Centre's compliance with Standard 23 — ensuring that patients diagnosed with specific forms of PAH (idiopathic, heritable, anorexigen-induced, or connective tissue disease-associated) receive drug therapy within 12 weeks of their first referral — has shown a concerning downward trajectory.

Figure 1: Sheffield's Standard 23 compliance dropped below the 80% threshold in 2018-19 after two consecutive years of meeting the standard.

The 8 percentage-point decline over the two-year period is substantial. The centre acknowledges this regression in its planned actions, identifying the need for dedicated tracking staff and the appointment of an additional medical consultant in October 2019 to restore service capacity. These measures appear well-targeted to address the root causes of delay in the patient pathway.

It is notable that Sheffield was the only centre among the three audited reporting quantitative historical data for any standard. Royal Brompton and Newcastle provided qualitative descriptions without numerical benchmarks, limiting our ability to assess temporal trends at those sites.

Standard 35: A Shared Challenge Across All Centres

Perhaps the most striking pattern in this dataset is the universal engagement with Standard 35 — that 90% of patients undergoing pulmonary endarterectomy should wait less than four months from their diagnosis of chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH). All three centres reference this standard, and all three acknowledge that addressing it requires coordination with Royal Papworth Hospital, the national pulmonary endarterectomy centre.

Figure 2: Each centre faces distinct priority standards, but Standard 35 creates a collaborative requirement across all three PH centres.

Sheffield's Approach

Sheffield has taken the most proactive local measures: appointing an additional secretary to reduce administrative delays, assigning dedicated nursing staff to monitor the patient pathway, and specifically reviewing coronary angiography waiting times for accepted surgical patients. They are also collaborating directly with Royal Papworth.

Royal Brompton & Newcastle

Both centres explicitly defer to Royal Papworth's national response. Royal Brompton supplements this with local pathway tracking and regular audits, while expressing interest in a virtual MDT model to accelerate surgical suitability decisions. Newcastle provides no actionable detail beyond the reference.

Insight: Standard 35 reveals a systemic bottleneck in the CTEPH surgical pathway. While individual PH centres can optimise their own processes, the ultimate determinant of wait times lies at the single national referral centre (Royal Papworth). This suggests the audit's recommendations would benefit from being escalated to a system-wide review of referral volume, surgical capacity planning, and triage protocols at Royal Papworth.

Patient Pathways Identified

The action plans collectively identify three distinct patient pathway segments where delays occur:

Referral → Diagnosis (Std 19)

Patients whose referral letters arrived over six months ago must have a diagnosis recorded. Newcastle's Freeman Hospital monitors all new patient activity to ensure timely documentation.

Referral → Drug Therapy (Std 23)

PAH drug therapy must commence within 12 weeks. Sheffield's declining performance indicates bottlenecks between referral receipt and treatment initiation, likely in assessment or capacity-constrained consultation scheduling.

Diagnosis → Surgery (Std 35)

The most complex pathway, spanning from CTEPH diagnosis through pulmonary endarterectomy at Royal Papworth Hospital. This involves multiple handoffs: local PH centre assessment → national surgical referral → MDT decision → surgery scheduling → pre-operative investigations (e.g., coronary angiography).

Recommendations

1. Escalate Standard 35 to System-Wide Review

All three centres point to Royal Papworth Hospital as the critical node in the pulmonary endarterectomy pathway. The NAPH audit team should commission a separate review of Royal Papworth's CTEPH surgical capacity and referral processing times. Virtual MDT proposals from Royal Brompton should be fast-tracked.

2. Prioritise Sheffield Standard 23 Recovery

Sheffield's 8-percentage-point decline is the clearest quantified deterioration signal. The planned consultant appointment and dedicated tracking staff are appropriate responses. However, these actions were set for implementation in 2020, meaning the next annual report will show whether recovery has begun. An interim checkpoint at 6 months would be advisable.

3. Standardise Quantitative Reporting Across Centres

Only Sheffield provides historical quantitative performance data. Royal Brompton and Newcastle should be encouraged to submit baseline and current-measure figures alongside their narrative action plans. This enables the NAPH programme to track improvement trajectories nationally and identify centres needing external intervention.

4. Publish Cross-Centre Comparative Benchmarks

With sufficient data, the NAPH programme could publish anonymised comparative benchmarks for each standard. Knowing that one centre achieves 92% on drug therapy initiation while another sits at 79% creates productive peer pressure and enables sharing of best practices.

Methods & Limitations

Data source: Three markdown-formatted Local Action Plan documents from the NAPH 10th Annual Report cycle, covering Sheffield, Royal Brompton, and Newcastle PH centres.

Approach: Textual data was extracted from structured tables within each document, capturing Standard ID, descriptive text, recommendation text, and planned actions. Quantitative performance figures (available only for Sheffield/Standard 23) were parsed from the narrative text using pattern matching.

Limited scope of quantitative analysis: Only Sheffield reported time-series performance data (three years), enabling trend analysis. Royal Brompton and Newcastle provided only qualitative descriptions. Consequently, the statistical analysis is necessarily skewed toward Sheffield's experience.

Cross-referencing limitation: Multiple centres reference "see Royal Papworth response" for Standard 35, indicating a centralised reporting structure. Without access to Royal Papworth's own LAP submission, assessments of compliance against Standard 35 remain incomplete.

Visualization approach: Charts were created using Python's matplotlib library. Where exact numerical values were not provided (Stds 19, 35), illustrative relative scores were used in comparative visualisations to convey qualitative assessment of each centre's engagement level.
