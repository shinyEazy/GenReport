What Matters Most

1. Surgery wait-time delays are a universal challenge. All five PH centres flag Standard 35 (PEA wait < 4 months for ≥90% of patients) in their action plans. Four centres explicitly defer to the Royal Papworth Hospital national reference response, indicating this is a system-wide bottleneck rather than a local failings issue.
2. Sheffield's drug therapy initiation has slipped below standard. Sheffield reported 79% compliance in 2018–19, down from 87% (2016–17) and 86% (2017–18). The target requires 80%. Their action plan calls for dedicated pathway tracking and an additional medical consultant, appointed October 2019.
3. Imperial's issue was documentation, not practice. Their Standard 23 non-compliance stemmed from cardiac catheterisation being performed externally but not recorded internally. The fix is a quarterly review process — no change in clinical practice is needed.
4. Improvement actions cluster around capacity building and tracking. The most common intervention type across centres is patient pathway monitoring/tracking, followed by staffing increases and audit/review mechanisms.

Standards Performance Benchmarks

⚠️ Standard 35 (PEA wait times): Across all five centres, this is the most frequently flagged standard. The requirement that 90% of patients wait less than four months from CTEPH diagnosis to pulmonary endarterectomy depends heavily on coordination with the single national referral centre at Royal Papworth Hospital. This makes it a systemic constraint that individual PH centres cannot fully resolve independently.
ℹ️ Standard 23 variability: Sheffield (page 23) references the 80% drug-therapy-initiation target, while Imperial (also page 23 in their own document) references a different Standard 23 requiring 95% cardiac catheterisation documentation before treatment. These refer to two distinct quality metrics that happen to share a page number in their respective reports.
✅ Newcastle's Standard 19 (95% diagnosis recording): A clear, measurable target with a straightforward remediation strategy — systematic monitoring of patient activity. No specific performance figure is given, suggesting this may be either a new focus area or a retrospective correction.

Recommended Next Steps

  1. System-level engagement on Standard 35: Given that all five centres defer to Royal Papworth for PEA wait times, NAPH should coordinate a cross-centre workstream to engage NHS England on capacity constraints at the national referral centre.
  2. Follow up on Sheffield's trajectory: With performance falling from 87% to 79%, confirm whether the October 2019 consultant hire and pathway-tracking role have reversed this trend in the next reporting cycle.
  3. Share documentation best practices: Imperial's resolution (external catheters not logged) is likely a common pattern. Propose a standardised protocol for capturing external procedure data across all centres.
  4. Track Newcastle's Standard 19 outcomes: Without a baseline compliance figure, it is hard to measure progress. Recommend establishing a baseline measurement alongside the monitoring programme.
  5. Consolidate similar standards: Standards on pages 23 and 28 describe related but numerically distinct targets. Harmonising these descriptions would improve cross-centre comparability.

Methods and Limitations

This analysis extracted action-item level data from five markdown-formatted LAP documents. Each document contained only a subset of its total standards — typically 1–2 per centre. Centres that met all standards may have excluded those items from their action plan tables entirely, so this analysis reflects areas of concern, not overall centre performance. Page numbers referenced correspond to each centre's individual report; identical page numbers do not necessarily indicate the same standard across centres.
