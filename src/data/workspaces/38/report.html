⚡ Key Findings at a Glance

  • Golden Jubilee stands out as the only centre not meeting standards: It fails on both Drug Therapy Commencement (80% target, within 12 weeks) and Endarterectomy Wait (90% target, <4 months), requiring active intervention.
  • Pulmonary endarterectomy wait time is the most contested standard: Three of four centres are referred to Royal Papworth Hospital for coordination — highlighting a system-wide bottleneck beyond individual centres' control.
  • Royal Brompton and Imperial each meet all their assigned standards: Both have concrete action plans in place (virtual MDT tracking for Brompton, quarterly documentation review for Imperial).
  • Newcastle monitors diagnosis recording but references Royal Papworth for endarterectomy: Their current priority is ensuring diagnoses are recorded within 6 months of referral receipt.
  • Action plan quality varies significantly: Some centres provide specific, measurable next steps, while others simply defer to "See Royal Papworth response."

🔗 Deep Dive: Pulmonary Endarterectomy Wait Time (Page 35)

Standard 35 appears in every single centre's LAP, making it the most universally problematic standard. The requirement is that 90% of patients undergoing pulmonary endarterectomy should wait less than 4 months from diagnosis of CTEPH.

Centre Status Action Taken
Royal Brompton Met Local tracking + virtual MDT with Royal Papworth proposed to speed MDT decisions on surgical suitability
Newcastle Monitoring "See Royal Papworth response" — cross-centre coordination required
Golden Jubilee Not Met "See Royal Papworth response" — no independent pathway exists locally
Imperial Met "See Royal Papworth response" — but still meets the 90% target through effective referral management

Systemic dependency: Three centres explicitly defer to Royal Papworth for this standard. This reveals that Standard 35 is fundamentally a network-level challenge rather than a local one — individual PH centres cannot independently address delays because they depend on the centralised endarterectomy service at Royal Papworth Hospital. A joint England-wide pathway redesign may be needed.

💡 Recommendations for the 11th Annual Report Cycle

  • Prioritise Golden Jubilee intervention: The centre has two unmet standards. Monitor their right heart catheterisation throughput improvements closely. If the 11th Report still shows non-compliance, escalate with a formal root-cause analysis.
  • Network-level endarterectomy pathway reform: Since 75% of centres still struggle with Standard 35 despite deferring to Royal Papworth, advocate for a nationally coordinated CTEPH patient journey with SLAs and automated wait-time tracking.
  • Replicate Imperial's documentation fix: Their approach of distinguishing between externally performed procedures and internal records could solve similar issues at other centres where "missing" data is actually "misplaced" data.
  • Standardise language in action plans: Replace "See Royal Papworth response" with specific milestones (e.g., "Submit referral by Q3", "Track waiting times monthly") to enable meaningful benchmarking across centres.
  • Establish early-warning indicators: Implement real-time dashboards so centres can flag potential standard breaches well before the annual report deadline rather than reacting post-hoc.

⚙ Methods & Limitations

Data Source: This analysis is based on the National Audit of Pulmonary Hypertension (NAPH) Local Action Plan documents from four NHS hospitals for the 10th Annual Report cycle: Royal Brompton, Newcastle (Freeman Hospital), Golden Jubilee, and Imperial.

Methodology: Each centre's LAP was parsed to extract standards referenced, targets defined, and planned actions described. Compliance was assessed using three categories: Met (explicit commitment with corrective action completed or ongoing effectively), Not Met (explicitly failing to meet standard with insufficient action identified), and Monitoring/Pending (active improvement programme with unclear near-term outcome).

Limitations:

  • LAP documents are self-reported and describe planned actions rather than verified outcomes. Actual compliance rates are not provided in these excerpts.
  • Each centre addresses only 1–2 standards in these excerpts; some standards may appear multiple times (e.g., Standard 35 across all four centres), which inflates apparent coverage.
  • The "Monitoring" category captures ambiguity in self-assessment — a centre marked as "monitoring" may already be compliant or still far off target.
  • No quantitative metrics (actual percentages, wait times, patient counts) are included in these excerpts, limiting statistical depth.
