# National Audit of Pulmonary Hypertension — Local Action Plan (10th Annual Report)
## Sheffield PH Centre Compliance Analysis

<p align="center"><em>Report date: August 10, 2026 | Source: NAPH LAP 10AR v1.0 Sheffield for web.md</em></p>

---

## What Matters Most

1. **Compliance has slipped below the national standard.** The PAH drug therapy initiation rate fell from 87% (2016–17) to 79% (2018–19), missing the ≥80% threshold set by the audit.

2. **Two critical standards are under review** — one governing timely drug therapy initiation for PAH patients, the other managing surgical wait times for pulmonary endarterectomy in CTEPH patients.

3. **Seven planned actions have been committed** across both standards, including dedicated staffing, capacity reviews, new consultant appointments, and closer collaboration with Royal Papworth Hospital (the national pulmonary endarterectomy centre).

4. **Resource constraints appear to be the primary driver.** Both action plans cite administrative delays, insufficient nursing/secretarial capacity, and limited service availability as root causes.

## Standard 1: PAH Drug Therapy Initiation — Performance Decline

### The finding: performance is dropping year on year, crossing below the national benchmark

The target is that at least 80% of new referrals presenting with idiopathic, heritable, anorexigen-induced, or connective tissue disease-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), who are in WHO functional class II, III, or IV, should commence PAH-specific drug therapy within 12 weeks of receiving their first referral.

Performance over the three audited years shows a clear downward trend:

| Year | Performance | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 2016–17 | 87% | ✅ Met |
| 2017–18 | 86% | ✅ Met |
| 2018–19 | 79% | ❌ Not Met |

The 8-percentage-point decline between 2016–17 and 2018–19 represents the most significant metric in this report. Falling below the 80% threshold in 2018–19 triggered the recommendation that the PH centre must review its patient pathway.

### Root causes identified

The action plan identifies several contributing factors:
- **No dedicated staff member tracking patients** through the referral-to-treatment pathway
- **Service capacity limitations**, partially attributed to high case volume
- **Administrative bottlenecks** in processing referrals

### Interventions underway (planned for 2020)

1. Appoint a **dedicated staff member** to track patients through the pathway
2. Conduct a **service capacity review**
3. Appoint an **additional medical consultant** (effective October 2019)

These are appropriate structural fixes targeting the bottleneck directly. However, the report does not specify a measurable timeline for when the 80% target will be recovered, nor does it present interim monitoring metrics.

## Standard 2: Pulmonary Endarterectomy Wait Times — A Systemic Challenge

### The finding: no historical performance data is available, but systemic delays are acknowledged

The standard requires that ≥90% of patients undergoing pulmonary endarterectomy should wait less than four months from diagnosis of CTEPH. Unlike Standard 1, this row contains no numerical performance history, suggesting either:

- The data was still being collected during this reporting cycle, or
- The centre's baseline figures did not yet meet the threshold enough to justify inclusion

Regardless, the complexity of the patient pathway—spanning referral, initial assessment, coronary angiography scheduling, and transfer to the national centre at Royal Papworth Hospital—is acknowledged as a multi-layered challenge requiring NHS England and the PH centre to act jointly.

### Interventions underway

Four distinct actions have been committed:

| # | Action | Priority Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work with Royal Papworth Hospital to reduce time to surgery | System-level coordination |
| 2 | Appoint 1 additional secretary | Administrative efficiency |
| 3 | Dedicated nursing staff monitoring the pathway | Real-time visibility |
| 4 | Review waiting times to coronary angiography | Surgical prerequisites |

This standard addresses a rarer but more lethal condition (CTEPH carries high untreated mortality). Even without performance data, the proactive interventions demonstrate awareness of systemic risk. The involvement of the national specialist centre (Royal Papworth) is appropriately placed given the highly specialised nature of pulmonary endarterectomy surgery.

Both standards share a common theme: **administrative and staffing resource constraints**. Neither appears to stem from clinical decision-making failures but rather from operational capacity gaps.

## Gaps and Limitations

- **No 2019–20 or later data**: The latest reported figure is from 2018–19. There is no evidence presented that the committed interventions have already taken effect or improved results.
- **No numeric targets for recovery**: The plan states what will be done but does not quantify expected improvement (e.g., "target ≥85% by end of 2020").
- **Standard 2 lacks baseline data**: Without current performance figures for the pulmonary endarterectomy standard, compliance cannot be assessed.
- **Sample scope**: This document covers only 2 of the many standards evaluated in the full NAPH audit. Other centres' performance is not included for comparison.
- **Case mix unknown**: The actual number of patients served each year is not provided, which limits understanding of whether low volume could affect percentages.

---

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Request updated performance data for 2019–20 and 2020–21** to assess whether the consultant appointment and dedicated tracker role reversed the declining trend.
2. **Set a recovery target for Standard 1**: e.g., ≥85% by mid-2020 with quarterly monitoring.
3. **Collect and publish Standard 2 baseline data** so that the Royal Papworth collaboration can be evaluated objectively.
4. **Monitor coronary angiography wait times separately**, as this is a specific bottleneck identified within the endarterectomy pathway.
5. **Benchmark against other UK PH centres** participating in the national audit to determine whether Sheffield's experience reflects a system-wide issue or a localised problem.
6. **Consider patient safety incident review** if any individual patients waited beyond clinically acceptable thresholds during the period of non-compliance.
