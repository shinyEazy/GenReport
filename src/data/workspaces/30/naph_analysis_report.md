### Newcastle / Freeman Hospital — Diagnosis Recording Delays (Page 19)

Newcastle addresses a standard requiring that ≥95% of patients referred over six months ago should have a diagnosis recorded. Their planned action commits to monitoring all new patient activity and ensuring timely diagnosis entry. This suggests the issue is operational rather than clinical — likely related to administrative throughput or database completion practices.

### Imperial — Documentation Errors Masking Compliance (Page 23)

Imperial's response for cardiac catheterization documentation before treatment is revealing: after reviewing cases flagged as non-compliant, they found that catheterization had actually been performed but completed externally, meaning the database simply did not capture the information. Their proposed fix is quarterly review of standards and closer-to-real-time identification of discrepancies.

This finding has broader implications: **reported non-compliance rates may understate true clinical compliance** when external providers' records are not integrated into the audit database.

## Systemic Observations

1. **National referral dependency is widespread**: Three of four centres defer on the most resource-intensive standard (pulmonary endarterectomy), highlighting the structural limitation of regional PH centres lacking surgical capacity. Centralisation at Royal Papworth creates coordination challenges that local centres cannot resolve independently.

2. **Measurement transparency varies dramatically**: Only Sheffield reports longitudinal trends, enabling stakeholders to see whether interventions work. Without before-and-after benchmarks, the impact of all other planned actions remains unquantified.

3. **Data quality is itself a quality metric**: Imperial's case demonstrates how database incompleteness can distort compliance metrics. Other centres may face similar issues where treatments performed elsewhere go unrecorded.

4. **No cross-centre benchmarking visible**: Despite being part of a national audit, none of the centres reference peer performance data in their action plans. There is an opportunity to share best practices — particularly around Sheffield's dedicated-tracker model.

## Methods & Limitations

**Data source**: Four NAPH Local Action Plan documents (Markdown format) representing 10th Annual Reports from Sheffield, Royal Brompton, Newcastle, and Imperial hospitals.

**Parsing approach**: Pipe-delimited table rows were extracted and structured into tabular records. Standard text was parsed to identify key numeric thresholds (80%, 90%, 95%). HTML tags in action texts were stripped during processing.

**Limitations**:
- This analysis covers only four hospitals out of all NAPH participating centres
- Action plan word count is a proxy for detail level, not necessarily for quality or feasibility
- Performance data spans 2016–19 only; no more recent data was available in these documents
- Standard category assignments are based on keyword matching against standard text and may not capture nuanced overlaps between categories
- The analysis does not include outcome follow-up data — whether planned actions successfully improved performance
