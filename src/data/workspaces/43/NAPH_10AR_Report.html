What Matters Most

1. Quality of Care Is Largely Meeting Targets

Eleven of thirteen national standards were met at the national level in 2018–19. Diagnostic timeliness (99%), cardiac catheterization compliance (99%), and quality of life recording (94%) all exceeded their targets by comfortable margins.

2. PEA Waiting Times Remain the Critical Failure Point

Only 23% of patients undergoing pulmonary endarterectomy waited less than four months from diagnosis, far short of the 90% target. This is the single weakest performing standard across every centre.

3. Comorbidities Halve Five-Year Survival

Five-year cumulative survival for IPAH without comorbidities was 54.1%, compared to just 26.9% for those with additional conditions. This 27-percentage-point gap represents one of the strongest prognostic differentiators in the dataset.

4. Treatment Intensifies With Severity

Dual or triple combination therapy was prescribed for 62% of IPAH patients without comorbidities, but only for 43% of those with comorbidities, where mono-therapy remains dominant (56%). This reflects both clinical judgment and disease complexity.

Trends Over Time (2015–16 to 2020–21)

Figure 2: Trajectory of each national standard across six consecutive audit years.

The clearest positive trend is NS 4 (seen or discharged within 30 days), which climbed from 47% in 2015–16 to 100% by 2020–21. This suggests that service redesigns across centres have been effective at reducing initial assessment delays.

NS 8 (drug therapy initiation within 12 weeks) shows an oscillatory pattern: rising from 76% to 82%, then dipping to 61%, and recovering to 89%. The variation likely reflects capacity constraints introduced during periods of rapid patient volume growth.

NS 13 remains stubbornly flat around 11–23%, reflecting systemic bottlenecks at the single national PEA centre (Royal Papworth).

Survival by WHO Functional Class

From the supplementary survival analysis document, patients in WHO functional class II at diagnosis show markedly better outcomes. For IPAH without comorbidities in class II, five-year survival reaches approximately 55%, compared with ~45% for class III and ~48% for class IV. These figures underscore the importance of accurate functional classification at presentation.

Gender Distribution

Women predominate across most IPAH subgroups: 65% of IPAH patients overall are female, rising to 76% among the youngest quintile (ages 18–41). This ratio contrasts sharply with CTEPH, where operated patients are 55% male and non-operated patients are 46% female.

First-Line Drug Choice

For newly eligible PAH patients, phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitors remain the first-line choice in 89% of cases (NS 10, target 80%). This has been stable since 2015–16 (90%), indicating consistent adherence to international treatment guidelines.

Areas Where Centres Diverge

  • PEA waiting times (NS 13) are uniformly poor across all adult centres. Even the best performers (Golden Jubilee, Royal Papworth at 23%) fall far short of the 90% target. This bottleneck sits outside centre control at the single national PEA surgery provider.
  • Therapy initiation (NS 8) shows the widest centre-to-centre variation, from 61% (Golden Jubilee) to 91% (Newcastle). Golden Jubilee’s Local Action Plan specifically addresses this by increasing diagnostic right heart catheterisation throughput.
  • Diagnostic timeliness (NS 3) is excellent overall: seven of eight centres exceed 95%, with only Great Ormond Street (89%) falling below. The children’s centre’s lower score may reflect case mix rather than service failure.
  • Cardiac catheterisation (NS 5) is nearly universal (≥92% at every centre), demonstrating strong compliance with pre-treatment verification standards.

Recommendations for Service Improvement

  1. Accelerate PEA surgical capacity. NS 13 is the only standard failing at the national level, and every centre reports zero to marginal compliance. Commissioners should prioritise investment in Royal Papworth’s surgical capacity and explore pathway innovations such as virtual MDT decision-making described by several centres.
  2. Reduce therapy initiation delays at high-burden centres. Golden Jubilee (61%) and Royal Papworth (75%) lag significantly behind peers on NS 8. Targeted investment in right heart catheterisation capacity and pathway co-ordination staff has already shown promise at Sheffield (79% and improving toward 89%).
  3. Strengthen comorbidity assessment. The 27-percentage-point survival gap between IPAH with and without comorbidities warrants earlier and more systematic screening for overlapping respiratory and cardiovascular disease. centres should consider formalised comorbidity scoring at initial presentation.
  4. Maintain momentum on documentation standards. While NS 3 through NS 12 are all met, vigilance is essential. Historical data show that some standards (particularly NS 8) can regress quickly during periods of patient volume growth. Quarterly review cycles, as piloted at Imperial College, should become the norm.
  5. Expand Northern Ireland participation. All eight designated specialist centres now participate, but Northern Ireland remains excluded from the audit. Reintegration would complete national coverage and improve generalisability of benchmarking results.

Methods & Limitations

Data Sources

  • NAPH 10th Annual Report Open Data (CSV): 91,749 rows covering appendices survival curves, group characteristics, national standards summaries, reference tables, and survival analysis data across six audit years (2015–16 to 2020–21).
  • Main Report Markdown: Narrative context, standard definitions, methodology notes, and quoted clinician commentary.
  • Supplementary Survival Analysis Markdown: Kaplan-Meier curves and annual survival tables stratified by sex, age quintile, WHO functional class, and IPAH subtype.
  • Local Action Plans (5 centres): Qualitative data on planned improvements linked to specific standards.

Methodological Notes

  • Survival curves are computed from the earliest diagnosis date for each patient’s latest diagnosis; 98% of patients maintain the same PH diagnosis across follow-up.
  • Survival curves are truncated when the patient count drops below 50 to prevent artificial flattening from unconfirmed mortality status.
  • Scottish centre (Golden Jubilee) patients cannot be traced at the Office for National Statistics or National Records of Scotland due to pseudonymisation; therefore Scottish-specific survival statistics are excluded from lung disease/left heart disease analyses.
  • National Standard values are rounded to the nearest whole number before comparison with targets.
  • Open Data values have been cross-checked against published tables in the main report; minor discrepancies (≤2 percentage points) arise from rounding and updated methodology applied retrospectively.

Limitations

  • The open data CSV provides aggregated percentages rather than individual-level records; multivariable survival modelling is not feasible with the available data.
  • Centre-level comparisons are limited to four standards with sufficient cross-centre coverage; finer-grained benchmarks (e.g., specific outcome measures, complication rates) are not included in the published open data.
  • Local action plans represent self-reported intentions rather than audited outcomes; achievement rates against these plans are unknown.
  • The audit primarily captures patients managed at designated specialist centres; community-managed patients or those lost to follow-up prior to referral are under-represented.
